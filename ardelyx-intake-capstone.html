Designing the front door to a company’s PMO

Ardelyx’s departments were starting technology work that IT found out about too late. Our team designed the intake and initiation process every new request goes through first, so it’s seen, sized, owned and decided before anyone commits money, vendors or engineers.

The problem: IT was the last to know

Departments at Ardelyx had grown used to buying and running their own technology. As the company scaled, that meant duplicate tools, surprise demands on a small group of IT specialists, and vendor work nobody had defined clearly.

How a request moved before

  1. A department picks a vendor and starts planning.
  2. Requirements turn out to need infrastructure, security and data access.
  3. IT hears about it after commitments are made.
  4. Specialists get pulled off other work; schedules slip.
  5. Nobody checks whether a similar tool was already licensed.

How it moves through our model

  1. One 5–10 minute form captures the request and gives it an ID.
  2. It’s tiered by size, risk and complexity.
  3. Existing tools and shared specialists are checked up front.
  4. A named sponsor, owner and funding are attached.
  5. A cross-functional IT group decides on a regular cadence and tells the requester why.

      How the team got better

      We ran a retrospective every module and turned the findings into owned, dated actions.

      Module 1

      Organizing the team

      • Formally restructured roles after a member left, instead of quietly spreading the load.
      • Comment before editing someone else’s section.
      • Meeting reminders and fixed time boxes.
      Module 2

      Writing requirements

      • MoSCoW prioritization and testable “shall” statements.
      • Trace every requirement to case evidence or a sponsor statement.
      • WhatsApp for quick questions, Teams for documents.
      Module 3

      Tightening the design

      • Reassess all 33 Must-have requirements; too many were marked Must.
      • Discuss numeric thresholds earlier.
      • Walk five cases through the draft before formal validation.

      Actions from Module 3 got owners and dates: a shared requirements status list (Nov 2), a MoSCoW reassessment (Nov 6), D6/D7 coordination (my action, with a teammate) and case walk-throughs before Nov 13.

      What’s real and what isn’t

      Real

      • The project plan, WBS, schedule, cost estimate, interface agreements and retrospectives, all written by our team.
      • The sponsor’s direction on Oct 1 that changed the design from a visibility log to a formal but lightweight approval step.
      • My contributions as recorded in the IPP version history and Oct 26 health check.

      Not real, or not yet

      • This is a university capstone built on a business case; we design and validate the model, and Ardelyx would implement it.
      • No live requests have gone through it, and no review group has been staffed.
      • Budget figures are plan estimates, not actual spend.
      • The project is in progress; final acceptance is planned for Dec 11.
