Capstone · Ardelyx IT transformation · in progress

Designing the front door to Ardelyx’s IT

At Ardelyx, a growing biopharma, departments bought and started technology projects on their own, and IT often found out last. Our four-person team is designing an intake operating model: one front door where every new technology request is captured, sized, owned and decided before money, vendors or engineers are committed.

My part

The team rotated the lead role; I led the third version of our Integrated Project Plan (IPP v3.0) and the Interface & Dependency Agreement across four partner workstreams, and owned the design inputs for deliverables D5, D6 and D9.

Initiation01

The problem: IT was the last to know

With no single entry point, a request moved ahead on the business side, a vendor was picked and commitments were made before IT was involved. IT then inherited the consequences and schedules slipped.

Before · no common entry point
1Department has an idea
2Vendor is picked
3Money and commitments are made
4IT finds out
5Schedules slip
After · our intake model
1Request logged with an ID
2Tiered and checked
3Sponsor and funding named
4IT review decides
5Routed to delivery

Root cause: requests had no ID, no tier, no named owner, and no point where IT could weigh in before commitments. The sponsor asked for a more mature IT operating model, without the bureaucracy of a much bigger company.
