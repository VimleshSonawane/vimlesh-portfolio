Getting new customers live in 10 days instead of 21

Before a business can use the software it has bought, its account must be set up, its data imported, its security reviewed and its team trained. That took 21 days. As project manager, I planned a guided onboarding workflow with a clear owner for every step, delivered it across three two-week sprints, and tested it in a pilot, with a target of 10 days.

01

The problem: a disconnected process nobody owned

New B2B customers took 21 days to go live after buying the product. The onboarding steps were spread across several checklists, progress depended on email follow-ups, no one owned each step, a security review could hold up launch, and neither the customer nor the team had one view of progress. Customers were paying for software they could not yet use.

Meet Maya, a hypothetical new customer. Follow her through the old process below, then through the new one.

08

The pilot: what testing changed

In Sprint 3, I ran a simulated pilot with 2–3 sample customer and partner profiles. Two findings led to two refinements.

Finding 1 · usability

Steps didn’t say who should act next

BeforeSteps listed without an owner
→
RefinedEach step marked as a customer action or an internal action
Finding 2 · usability

Import errors were written in technical terms

BeforeTechnical error messages
→
RefinedPlain-language guidance for common import errors

Expected benefit: fewer support requests from customers (analysis).

Security was planned, not discovered. The RAID log already called for Security/Legal involvement from Sprint 1 and a review before pilot go-live.
